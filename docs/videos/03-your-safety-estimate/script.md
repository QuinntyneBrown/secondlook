# 03 · Your safety estimate

The call is over, and you want one thing: a number. This video walks through your report. It covers the estimate to get the car safety certified, what is behind it, and how to use it to decide.

## Getting the report

Your report arrives within an hour of the call, by email and as a notification. If the body shop review is still being finished, the mechanic's estimate is already there, and we tell you when the rest is ready. It is usually about thirty minutes behind the mechanic's.

## The estimate

At the top is the number that matters: the estimated cost to get the car safety certified. Here it is one thousand one hundred eighty to one thousand five hundred forty dollars. Underneath, we add the asking price of eight thousand nine hundred dollars, so you see the all-in cost: ten thousand eighty to ten thousand four hundred forty.

## The findings

Below that is every finding, each with its own price range, and they add up to the estimate at the top. Required repairs are what the car needs to pass: here, the front brake pads and rotors, and the rear tires. Recommended repairs are worth doing but not required to pass, such as the control arm bushing and a windshield chip. Anything that passes says so.

## The body shop

If you added a body shop review, it sits in its own section, with its own repair estimate. Here the reviewer found surface rust on the rocker panels, and a bumper repaint that suggests a past repair, with no structural damage seen. Body repairs are priced separately from the safety certification estimate.

## The decision

Then comes the question: is it worth it? We show the math, and then it is your call. To compare, put your reports side by side, lowest all-in cost first. Or add another car in the same window for twenty five dollars. These costs are estimates from each mechanic's call, and the cheapest car all-in is not always the best car.

## Recap

Here is what to remember. Your report arrives within an hour of the call. The headline is the cost to get the car safety certified, and adding the asking price gives you the all-in cost. Required repairs must be done to pass, and recommended ones are optional. Body repairs are priced separately. And the decision is always yours.
