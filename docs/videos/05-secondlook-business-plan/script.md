# 05 · The SecondLook business plan

Buying a used car as is? The asking price is only part of the story.

Most buyers can't get a mechanic out to see the car before the seller moves on. So they guess what it will cost to fix.

SecondLook puts a local mechanic on a live video call with the seller. The mechanic walks the car, and you get a written estimate of the work needed to get it safety certified.

Pricing is simple. Fifty dollars for your first inspection. Twenty-five dollars for each additional car in the same fourteen-day window. And twenty dollars to add a body shop review.

We start in the Greater Toronto Area, with budget-conscious buyers, first-time buyers, and people financing a car.

Growth starts with trusted referrals. Rodney's automotive relationships bring in the first partners: finance advisers, dealers, mechanics and body shops. We begin with three to five pilot partners.

Trust comes first. The buyer is the customer. Referral partners never shape the findings, and SecondLook never promises that a car will pass. It helps you decide what to do next.

The plan forecasts nearly forty-seven thousand dollars in revenue in year one, about one hundred seventy-nine thousand in year two, and about five hundred twelve thousand in year three.

That takes roughly seven hundred, twenty-six hundred, and seven thousand paid buying windows. Break-even is near five hundred a month, and year three turns slightly positive.

To get there, the plan asks for about one hundred ten thousand dollars, released in stages. Thirty-five thousand to launch and run a pilot. The rest only once the pilot proves demand and costs.

The first ninety days are a test. About sixty paid windows, measured costs, and real buyers who decide differently because of the report. If the numbers don't hold, we stop and adjust.

These are forecasts, not promises. Know the cost before you commit. SecondLook.
