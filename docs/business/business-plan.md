# SecondLook business plan

**Prepared:** 28 October 2026 · **Launch market:** Greater Toronto Area · **Currency:** Canadian dollars

**Planning horizon:** the first three full 12-month operating years after commercial launch. A launch date has not been assumed. This is a pre-launch management plan, not a report of historical performance, contracted sales or committed funding.

## Executive summary

SecondLook gives people buying a used car “as is” access to a local mechanic over live video. The mechanic guides a visual review and provides a written estimate of the work the vehicle may need to get safety certified. Buyers can add a body shop review and compare several cars before committing.

The proposed launch strategy is to build a trusted GTA referral network through Rodney's automotive relationships, recruit a small pool of qualified providers, and prove the service economics before expanding paid advertising. The user reports that Rodney has deep connections in the GTA car industry. His RTFG Financial website describes Ontario financial services including car loans, and its affiliate page describes an auto-finance offering. These sources support investigating the channel; they do not establish SecondLook partner commitments, buyer volumes or conversion rates. [RTFG Financial](https://www.rodneythefinanceguy.ca/), [partners and affiliates](https://www.rodneythefinanceguy.ca/financial-services/partners-affiliates).

The base plan reaches **$46,793 revenue in Year 1, $178,520 in Year 2 and $512,224 in Year 3**, after a 3% refund allowance and before HST. It requires 720, 2,574 and 6,930 paid buying windows respectively. The operating result turns positive in Year 3 at approximately **$20,763**, before corporate tax, depreciation, financing and working-capital timing. These are forecasts driven by explicit assumptions, not evidence of traction.

Plan for approximately **$110,000 of staged launch and operating funding**, subject to provider, insurance and development quotes. The model's annual cash-trough estimate is $91,911 including a $25,000 reserve; $110,000 adds about $18,000 for timing and execution variation. The base case also assumes the founder accepts below-market cash compensation in Years 1–2. Full founder replacement compensation would increase the capital needed.

## Customer and problem

The first customer is a GTA buyer considering an older, privately sold or explicitly as-is vehicle who cannot arrange an in-person mechanic visit before the seller moves on. Their question is practical: “What could this cost me beyond the asking price?”

Priority segments are budget-conscious buyers, first-time buyers who want a clear explanation, and finance-assisted buyers who need to understand repair costs alongside the vehicle purchase. These are target segments to validate, not established customer demographics. The service should remain available independently of financing and should not encourage buyers to borrow more than they can afford.

Customer discovery must verify that sellers will participate, buyers accept the limits of video, mechanics can provide a useful estimate remotely, and the written report changes a real purchase decision. A completed booking is more valuable evidence than a mailing-list signup.

## Service and pricing

| Offer                                        | Buyer price before applicable tax | What it provides                                 |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------: | ------------------------------------------------ |
| First mechanic inspection                    |                               $50 | Live review and written estimate for one vehicle |
| Additional vehicle in the same 14-day window |                               $25 | Another review and report for comparison         |
| Body shop add-on                             |               +$20 per inspection | Additional review of visible body condition      |

These prices and the 14-day window follow the current [pricing requirements](../specs/L2.md#l2-027-pricing-rules). A new purchase episode after the window expires starts again at $50. A paid window is an episode, not a subscription; the model does not assume annual customer retention or repeat subscription revenue. Standalone body-shop bookings are excluded until their offering and economics are settled.

The buyer books, provides vehicle details, arranges the seller's participation, pays and joins a guided call. The report records observed issues, estimated cost ranges and limitations. The product supports comparing vehicles using asking price plus estimated work. Reports must distinguish visible observations from items that require an in-person inspection.

SecondLook's remote assessment does not issue an Ontario Safety Standards Certificate or promise that a vehicle will pass. Ontario describes certificates as following a passed inspection at a licensed Vehicle Inspection Centre. Position SecondLook as help deciding whether to proceed to an in-person inspection or purchase. [Ontario certificate information](https://www.ontario.ca/page/safety-standards-certificate). The government search result was reviewed; direct access to that page returned 403 during research.

## Market entry and alternatives

Start with a reachable GTA buyer network rather than a percentage of Canada's entire used-car market. No verified count of GTA as-is transactions or Rodney's qualified buyers was supplied, so this plan does not invent a dollar-valued total addressable market.

The Year 1 reachable-demand assumption is 1,440 qualified partner referrals plus 1,440 direct qualified leads. At assumed conversions of 30% and 20%, those generate 432 partner windows and 288 direct windows. Year 3 requires 14,040 qualified partner referrals and 8,400 direct leads, producing 6,930 paid windows. Those acquisition volumes must be demonstrated before treating the growth curve as credible.

| Buyer alternative                    | Buyer benefit                                           | SecondLook's proposed place                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| In-person pre-purchase inspection    | Physical access, tools and tests unavailable over video | An earlier remote review when access or timing is difficult; escalate when physical examination is needed |
| Vehicle-history report               | Recorded vehicle history                                | Complement history with a guided look at the current vehicle; neither proves the other complete           |
| Advice from a seller or acquaintance | Easy access and often low cost                          | A consistent review and written estimate from a qualified independent provider                            |
| Buying without a review              | No inspection fee or scheduling                         | Make an informed decision about potential repair exposure before committing                               |

CARFAX Canada's buyer guide recommends pre-purchase inspection; that supports the customer need, not a claim that video replaces physical inspection. [CARFAX buyer guide](https://www.carfax.ca/learn/buying/used-car-buying-guide). Its search excerpt was reviewed; direct page access returned 403. Competitor prices, market shares and local transaction volumes have not been verified or used in the forecast.

## Rodney-led distribution

Rodney's proposed role is to lead relationship development: make introductions to finance professionals, buyer advisers, appropriate dealerships, mechanics and body shops, and help establish trust with early buyers. His title, time commitment, ownership and compensation remain to be agreed. Nothing in this plan represents an executed partnership with Rodney, RTFG Financial or an affiliate.

The first step is to map approximately 30 warm introductions and qualify 10–15 potential referral businesses. Start with three to five pilot partners; target an annual average of 10 active partners in Year 1, 30 in Year 2 and 65 in Year 3. The last two figures require expansion beyond Rodney's own personal referrals. An active partner should have a trained contact and a repeatable, consent-based referral process. Signed agreements and monthly cohort performance should establish actual productivity and attrition.

Give each partner a short explanation of the service, the 30-second pitch video, a tracked booking link and an example report. The moment of referral is after a buyer identifies a specific vehicle and before committing. Track unique qualified referrals, paid first bookings, completed reviews, refunds, additional cars, report quality and acquisition cost. Deduplicate buyers appearing through multiple partners or channels.

The model budgets a proposed **$5 acquisition fee per served partner window**, not per vehicle, repair job, loan approval or loan size. Payment terms and eligibility require agreement; the model conservatively budgets the cost even where subsequent refunds occur. Rodney's business-development compensation must be agreed within the founder/operations budget or added explicitly; the $5 fee is not also an undisclosed extra founder commission.

Avoid dependence on one distribution source. Track the share of windows coming from Rodney-controlled sources separately from the wider partner network. Once multiple partners are productive, target no individual referral business exceeding 20% of bookings; treat that as a proposed concentration limit, not current performance.

## Independence and trust

The buyer is the customer. A referral source must not influence findings, suppress a report, choose an agreeable mechanic or make compensation conditional on the car sale closing. Require conflict declarations where a provider is connected to the seller or referrer, and reassign the review when independence is compromised. Do not include repair or financing commissions in launch economics.

Use explicit consent for sharing contact information and reports. Financing and inspection decisions should stay separate; a SecondLook purchase must not imply financing approval, lender endorsement, certification or guaranteed savings. OMVIC's guidance addresses dealer advertising and as-is disclosures; it is relevant to how participating dealers describe the offer. Obtain professional review of the actual referral arrangements and customer wording before launch. [OMVIC advertising guideline](https://www.omvic.ca/selling/dealer-guidelines-and-resources/advertising-guideline/).

## Delivery and staffing

Launch with scheduled availability across the GTA, including evening and weekend blocks. Verify provider credentials, experience and insurance; use a consistent review checklist, report template, escalation process and customer support owner. Do not promise around-the-clock coverage at launch.

Planning assumptions allow 28 provider minutes for a first mechanic review, 20 for each additional car and 10 incremental body-shop minutes. These include the related review and report work and must be measured in pilot sessions. Assumed payouts are $22, $15 and $12 respectively. Providers have not agreed to these rates.

The model caps served windows at the calculated capacity, so excess demand does not automatically become revenue. Provider availability does not replace the need for qualified staff in each category.

The current repository is a pre-launch product scaffold with mocks and requirements. Complete and verify booking, payments, identity/access, call coordination, reports, refunds and provider administration before selling the automated service. A tightly managed manual pilot can validate demand and economics while development proceeds, provided its scope and limitations are clear to buyers.

### Unit economics and break-even

At Year 1 assumptions, a $50 mechanic inspection contributes approximately $22.81 after the refund allowance, $22 provider payout, payment costs and $1.75 variable service cost, before acquisition and fixed costs. A $25 additional-car inspection contributes approximately **$6.38** on the same basis. The body-shop add-on contributes approximately $6.74 incrementally, assuming its $12 payout and no separate payment transaction.

The reduced-price inspection is the most fragile part of the offer. A $5 increase in its provider payout cuts its contribution to roughly $1.38. If the pilot cannot deliver the additional inspection at the assumed cost and time, revise the offering through the normal product requirements process before scaling; do not hide the loss in the forecast.

With the modeled annual mix and acquisition spending, contribution per paid window rises from $19.92 to $22.22. Year 3 break-even is approximately **5,996 paid windows per year, or 500 per month**, compared with 6,930 forecast windows. That leaves a modest margin for error. At the Year 1 cost structure, break-even exceeds the small initial roster's capacity; Year 1 is deliberately a funded pilot rather than a self-financing operation.

## Main risks and decisions

| Risk                                                    | Response and decision                                                                                            |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provider payouts or duration exceed assumptions         | Measure each service separately; pause unprofitable offers before expanding                                      |
| Rodney's introductions do not create repeat paid demand | Track partner cohorts and qualified referrals; continue only where conversions and contribution justify the work |
| Referral relationships compromise buyer trust           | Keep findings independent; disclose conflicts and reassess provider assignment                                   |
| Video misses a costly or safety-critical defect         | Limit claims, record uncertainty, escalate to physical inspection and obtain appropriate insurance               |
| Sellers refuse calls or fail to attend                  | Confirm seller consent before scheduling; monitor failures and apply clear refund rules                          |
| Booking demand exceeds specialist availability          | Restrict slots and expand qualified capacity deliberately; do not sell unsupported turnaround promises           |
| Founder time is treated as free forever                 | Show replacement compensation and set a compensation plan before hiring or funding decisions                     |
| Costs are underquoted or liabilities uninsurable        | Obtain actual provider, insurance, technology and professional quotes before capital is released                 |

The next commercial decisions are Rodney's agreed involvement, the first named referral cohort, provider rate acceptance, the pilot launch budget and the measurable criteria for releasing the second funding tranche. No outreach, partnership representations or external commitments have been made as part of preparing this plan.
